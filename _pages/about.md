Welcome! I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Earth & Planetary Sciences Department at Johns Hopkins University. I work with Professor David Sing on spectroscopic observations of exoplanets and their atmospheres. I am driven by the desire to understand the diversity of atmospheres and surfaces on nearby worlds, and how host stars ultimately impact the fate of their planets.  

In particular, I am eager to understand whether most nearby terrestrial exoplanets have atmospheres, or whether we are surrounded by a vast expanse of barren rocks. To help answer this question, I am the PI of a JWST program to search for evidence of an atmosphere on the high-priority target LTT 1445 A b, as well as the PI of an HST program to characterize the near-ultraviolet flares of our closest neighbor, Proxima Centauri. In addition to these programs, I am co-I on multiple JWST programs studying the physics and chemistry of giant exoplanet atmospheres, which seek to investigate processes ranging from atmospheric dynamics to planet formation.

Prior to becoming an astronomer, I worked as an exercise physiologist at the University of Florida Health Shands Hospital. I ultimately returned to school to pursue my passion, receiving a master's degree in astronomy from Wesleyan University before joining the Earth & Planetary Sciences Department at JHU. I am eager to share my nontraditional story with others, in hopes of inspiring anyone who is dreaming of taking a chance.
